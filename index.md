## Quarter 2 Project Brainstorming

**1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

One of the most interesting topics we have explored this quarter is decoding visual stimulus orientation from neural activity using machine learning. I find it interesting that we can use the activity of populations of neurons to predict information about the stimuli being presented. Comparing models across different brain regions has also been interesting because it allows us to investigate which regions contain the most information about visual stimuli.

**2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

For my Quarter 2 Project, I would be interested in investigating how different machine learning models perform when predicting visual stimulus orientation from neural activity. In particular, I would like to compare linear and nonlinear models to determine whether more complex models can capture relationships that linear models miss. I would also investigate whether prediction accuracy varies across brain regions and how the number of neurons used affects model performance.

**3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

One change I would make is to experiment with a wider range of models and feature selection techniques. Our current approach involves linear regression and multinomial logistic regression with ridge regularization. While these provide useful baselines, I would be interested in investigating whether nonlinear models or dimensionality reduction techniques could improve performance. I would also consider using repeated cross-validation to evaluate how consistently the models generalize to unseen data.

**4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in exploring dimensionality reduction techniques such as PCA, t-SNE, and UMAP to better understand patterns in neural activity. I would also like to experiment with random forests or neural networks to determine whether nonlinear models offer improvements over traditional regression approaches. Additionally, I think visualizing differences in neural representations across brain regions could provide useful insights into how visual information is processed.
